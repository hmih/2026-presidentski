## The 2x2

| | good outcome | bad outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **sound process** | deserved success | **bad luck** |
| **weak process** | **luck** | deserved failure |

Botash is the clean case of *deserved failure*: a sealed 13-year take-or-pay commitment,
signed over the Christmas holidays, revisited only when parliament forced a renegotiation
mandate sixteen months later. Kyiv is the case the method exists for — **process and
outcome are not yet comparable**, because the outcome is still open. The drone refusal is
a weak-process decision whose outcome was partly repaired by the reversal, which is
exactly why it should not be scored as a failure on outcome alone.

Average 1.33; grade **weak**; outcome **bad** — EUR 512,000/day capacity payments, USD 360 m liability, contract unpublished and unratified; no documented benefit to show for it.

- **Adjudication (reversibility):** A scored 0, B scored 1. Kept at 0: a 13-year take-or-pay commitment to 2035, sealed and unratified, with no documented exit; B's own record cites ~BGN 3 bn exposure on rescission, which supports 0 rather than 1.

**Sensitivity:** If the sealed text contained a unilateral termination right at bounded cost, reversibility and design would rise and the average would reach 2.0 — the grade would flip to defensible.

**Evidence gaps:**
- No account by the signing ministers or the two chief executives of how the decision was taken appears in the fact-check dossiers; candour is judged from the public defences by political allies and later statements about the liability.
- No contemporaneous official statement by the decision-maker setting out the terms is in the record; candour is judged from the secrecy classification and from later political accounts.
- The contract text is unpublished and secret, so the term, the daily fee, the take-or-pay character and the absence of a termination clause are known only from official statements and leaks, not from the signed instrument.

- What alternative capacity or supply contracts were on the table in December 2022–January 2023, and at what price (brief) — this is why alternatives is scored from an absence of evidence, not from an established better option.

## Kyiv security agreement

*2026-03-30 — caretaker government of Andrey Gyurov*

| dimension | score | A / B |
|---|---|---|
| information | 2 | 2 / 2 |
| authority | 2 | 2 / 2 |
| alternatives | 2 | 2 / 1 |
| reversibility | 2 | 2 / 2 |
| design | 2 | 2 / 1 |
| candour | 2 | 2 / 1 |

Average 2.00; grade **defensible**; outcome **too_early** — Ten-year instrument, implementation pending; no outcome can yet be attributed to it.

- **Adjudication (alternatives):** B scored 1 on the view that the mandate route was available and not taken; A scored 2 for absence of a documented rejected option. Kept at 2: the route existed, but no deliberation record shows it was weighed.
- **Adjudication (design):** B scored 1 for the absence of milestones; A scored 2 for the implementation channel. Kept at 2, with the missing review mechanism recorded as a weakness.
- **Adjudication (candour):** B scored 1, A scored 2. Kept at 2: the substance was described accurately (no soldiers, no binding sentence), while the ratification claim and 'discussed in parliament' were not.

**Sensitivity:** If the Assembly had voted a mandate before signature, authority, information and alternatives would each rise and the grade would be sound; if an implementing contract had already been signed without any parliamentary step, candour and design would fall to 1 and the grade to weak.

**Evidence gaps:**
- Candour is inherently retrospective: the decision could not be scored on its author's contemporaneous account, which is not in the record, and is judged instead from the 29 October 2026 debate dossiers, as the method requires.
- No documented alternative option with a stated rationale exists in the record, which is why 'alternatives' is scored from an evidence gap (2) rather than as a discarded superior option.
- The agreement's legal effect is contested — Sofia calls it non-binding political commitments, Kyiv's Presidential Office described it as fixing 'clear obligations' — and no court ruling or authoritative legal opinion settles it.
- The instrument's legal effect is contested: Sofia calls it a non-binding political act outside the Vienna Convention while Kyiv's Presidential Office described it as fixing 'clear obligations', and no court ruling or authoritative legal opinion settles it (evidence open questions).
- What legal advice the caretaker government received on the instrument's status and on the need for ratification — not documented, so authority and information are assessed on the instrument's face and the speaker's stated position only.
- What legal advice the caretaker government received on the instrument's status and on whether a parliamentary mandate was required (brief).

## Refusal to join the NATO counter-drone programme

*2026-09-11 — Ministry of Defence*

| dimension | score | A / B |
|---|---|---|
| information | 1 | 1 / 1 |
| authority | 2 | 2 / 2 |
| alternatives | 2 | 2 / 2 |
| reversibility | 3 | 3 / 3 |
| design | 1 | 1 / 1 |
| candour | 1 | 3 / 1 |

Average 1.67; grade **weak**; outcome **mixed** — Reversed within six weeks (cabinet 28 October, note 30 October); the capability gap persisted through the period the refusal covered.

- **Adjudication (candour):** A scored 3, B scored 1 — the only disagreement of more than one point across all 18 dimension scores. Kept at 1: the stated ground shifted between 11 September ('we cannot afford two parallel initiatives') and 14 October ('at EUR 220,000 we can afford this thing'), and the government side's claim that the documents had been 'submitted' was premature, the cabinet approving only a draft note the day before the debate.

**Sensitivity:** If a pre-September ministry file showed a costed capability-gap assessment putting the national IRIS-T route at comparable cost, information and alternatives would rise and the grade would flip to defensible.

**Evidence gaps:**
- No NATO primary source (nato.int / NSPA) confirming the initiative's official name or Bulgaria's status; 'Drone Edge' (EUR 80 mln, 20 systems, participation since 2025) and the separate low-flying counter-drone memorandum with the EUR 220,000 fee are conflated in much of the coverage, so which programme the 11 September answer concerned is only inferable from the reported wording.
- No account by the minister or the government of why the refusal was made or how it was reviewed; candour is judged from his 11 September and 14 October statements and the 29 October debate interventions.
- The EUR 220,000 annual contribution is documented only through ministerial statements (BTA, BNR, Mediapool), not through a published decision text or budget annex - the 28 October protocol extract names the memorandum and the note but contains no figure.
- The alternatives dimension is scored 2 as neutral because the evidence does not establish a better option either way; a 'defensible' cap applies to unknown dimensions but is moot here.
- The full cost comparison between joining the NATO programme and the national alternative (brief) - this is why alternatives is scored from an absence of evidence, and the grade is capped at defensible.
- The full cost comparison between joining the NATO programme and the national alternative - listed in the brief as a known unknown and absent from the record.

## Assessor agreement

18 dimension scores compared across three decisions: 15 identical, 2 differing by one point,
1 differing by two (candour, drone refusal). The single major disagreement is adjudicated in
the dossier rather than averaged, and it is the most instructive one: one assessor read the
minister's shifting justification as accurate communication, the other as a moving target.
